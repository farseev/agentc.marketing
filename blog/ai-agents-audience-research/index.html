How AI agents do audience research without guessing

10 Aug 2026 · 6 min read · AgentC editorial team · FAQ

AI agents do reliable audience research when they read real audience conversations, cluster them into tensions and moments, and link every conclusion back to the posts that support it. Without that link, an agent is guessing fluently. AgentC's research agents run on SOMIN, an AI audience-research platform, so strategists can check the evidence before any finding reaches a brief.

Why do language models alone make poor researchers?

Ask a general language model what Gen Z thinks about budgeting apps and you will get a confident, articulate answer. It may even be partly right. But it is an answer assembled from training data of unknown age and origin, with no way to show you which real people said what. For a marketer, that is a problem. You cannot defend a strategy built on "the model said so".

Poor audience data quality is one of the most common complaints we hear from teams integrating AI. The models are not the bottleneck. The evidence is.

What does a research agent actually do?

Our research loop has four stages, run continuously rather than once per campaign.

  1. Gather: agents collect relevant public conversations for the brand's category, competitors and audiences.
  2. Decode: using SOMIN's AI agents, conversations are tagged for tone, emotion, topic and tension.
  3. Cluster: recurring tensions and moments are grouped, with the posts that evidence each one attached.
  4. Propose: agents suggest which tensions look most actionable for the brand, with reasons.

Then a person takes over. The conductor reviews the clusters, reads a sample of the underlying posts, and decides which tensions earn a brief.

Research terms we use

  • Tension: a conflict or pain point audiences voice repeatedly, for example wanting convenience but distrusting delivery fees.
  • Moment: a specific situation where that tension becomes acute, such as comparing prices at checkout.
  • Proposition: what the brand could credibly say or do in response.
  • Evidence link: the connection between a finding and the real posts behind it.

Why does traceability matter so much?

Three reasons.

It catches hallucination. If a cluster called "anxiety about hidden fees" turns out, on inspection, to be built from twelve posts about a single viral complaint, the strategist can see that and discount it.

It builds confidence. Marketers often feel pressure to present insight with certainty they do not have. Evidence they can open and read changes that conversation.

It respects audiences. Listening to what people say in public, at the level of shared themes, is a more dignified basis for marketing than tracking individuals. That fits the principle we organise around: prioritising humanity over efficiency.

An insight you cannot trace is an opinion with good formatting.

How do strategists check an agent's research?

We use a short review routine at every research checkpoint.

Research checkpoint routine

  • Read at least ten posts behind each proposed tension.
  • Ask whether the tension appears across different voices or just a loud few.
  • Check whether the language people use matches the agent's summary.
  • Reject tensions that are real but that the brand cannot credibly address.
  • Note what surprised you; surprises often become the best briefs.

How does research feed the rest of the loop?

Approved tensions flow directly to the creative loop, keeping the original language people used. That matters, because the most resonant copy often borrows audience phrasing. We describe what happens next in why AI creative needs a human edit.

Research also feeds search and AI visibility. The questions audiences voice in conversation are the questions they ask search engines and AI assistants. Our sister agency SNMRush uses the same SOMIN signal to build answer-ready content for search and AI answers.

What does good look like?

For an example of AI audience research applied to a consumer brand, the Keeta Hong Kong case study is a useful reference, and SOMIN's AI marketing strategy cases collect more. Good research does not produce a thicker deck. It produces a shorter list of tensions that a strategist believes in and can defend.

How often should the research loop run?

Continuously, but reviewed on a rhythm. Audience conversations shift with seasons, news, competitor moves and culture, so a research snapshot taken once a quarter is often stale before the campaign launches. Our agents gather and decode every day. The conductor reviews new or changing tensions weekly, and the whole picture monthly with the client.

The weekly review is where most value appears. A tension that was minor last month and is growing this month is often the best early signal a brand will get, well before it shows up in sales data or category reports. Equally, a tension that is fading tells you when to retire a message before it starts to feel out of touch.

What should research agents never do?

  • Profile or target named individuals from their public posts.
  • Present a finding without the evidence behind it.
  • Treat a single viral post as a trend.
  • Fill gaps in the data with a model's general knowledge, presented as research.

These rules are written into every research agent's charter at AgentC.

One more practical note. Research findings age, so every tension in our library carries the date it was last confirmed against fresh conversation. When a strategist builds a brief, they can see at a glance whether the evidence is from this week or from last season, and refresh it before committing budget. It is a small habit that prevents a surprising number of tone-deaf campaigns.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI agents do audience research on their own?

They can gather, decode and cluster conversations quickly, but a human strategist should review the evidence and decide which findings are credible and useful before they inform strategy.

What is an audience tension?

A conflict or pain point that audiences voice repeatedly, such as wanting convenience while distrusting extra fees. Tensions are strong starting points for creative briefs.

Why does AgentC use SOMIN for research?

SOMIN, an AI audience-research platform, links every conclusion to the real posts that support it, which lets strategists verify findings rather than trusting a model's summary.
